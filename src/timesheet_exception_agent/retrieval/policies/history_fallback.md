# Approving Time From Pay History

## When the coordinator uses history
If neither the manager nor the next-level manager responds in time, the coordinator approves the employee's time from their last two pay periods. The agent may propose these hours, but only the coordinator approves or enters them.

## What to copy from the last two periods
Use the employee's schedule, pay code, and hours from the last two closed pay periods. When both periods agree, those hours are the proposal. For a full-time hourly employee who worked 80 REG hours in each of the last two periods, propose 80 REG hours.

## Initials, timestamp, and notifying the manager
Every history-based approval is recorded with the coordinator's initials and a timestamp. The coordinator then notifies the manager of the hours entered. The manager can still object before payroll runs on Tuesday; objections after that are handled as retro adjustments.

## Manager asks the coordinator to use history
A reply saying the manager can't sign off today, for example while travelling without system access, and asking us to use the employee's usual hours is not an approval. Hand the exception to the coordinator at once; it does not wait for the 9:00 cut-off. The coordinator then works it from pay history as above, and the hand-off should say if that history is unreliable.

## When history is missing or unreliable
History is not reliable for a new hire with no closed periods, after a schedule change, when the last two periods disagree (for example 80 hours and then 64), or when recent hours are unusual. In these cases do not copy history. Ask a teammate, then fall back to standard hours for the job type.
