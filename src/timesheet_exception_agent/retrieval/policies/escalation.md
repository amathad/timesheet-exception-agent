# Escalation to the Next-Level Manager

## The escalation chain
The next-level manager is the manager's own manager. They are the only escalation step before the coordinator takes over. Some managers have no next-level manager; see the fallback policy for those cases.

## Manager on leave or out of office
Leave is the most common reason a manager is unavailable. If Teams shows the manager as out of office, or the manager replies that they are out, escalate immediately to the next-level manager. There is no need to wait for a follow-up time.

## Manager who does not reply
If the manager has not replied by the follow-up time, about 30 minutes after the first message, escalate to the next-level manager once.

## Manager who replies but has not decided by 9:00 AM
A manager who has replied, for example one stuck in meetings, is not escalated before 9:00 AM; they get reminders instead. If they still have not approved or corrected the time by 9:00 AM, escalate to the next-level manager at 9:00. This gives both managers ample time and still leaves the next-level manager their 30 minutes before the 10:00 AM lock.

## Next-level manager response time
The next-level manager is expected to reply within 30 minutes. Message them once. If they do not reply within 30 minutes, the exception passes to the coordinator without further messages. A later approval from them before the lock still resolves the exception.
