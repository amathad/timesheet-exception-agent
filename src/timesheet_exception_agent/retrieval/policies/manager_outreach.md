# Contacting Managers About Exceptions

States: ALL
Effective: 2026-01-01

## Who to contact first
The first contact for any exception is the employee's own manager, who is responsible for approving or correcting the time. Do not contact the employee directly about approval.

## Choosing email or Teams
Check the manager's Teams presence before writing. If the manager shows available, send a Teams message, which usually gets a faster answer. If they show away or their status is unknown, send an email. If Teams shows out of office, do not wait on the manager at all: follow the escalation policy.

## What the message should say
Keep messages short and polite. Name the employee, mention the 10:00 AM Monday lock, and ask only for what the exception needs: for unsubmitted time, ask that the time be submitted and approved; for unapproved time, ask for approval; for unusual hours, ask the manager to confirm or correct the hours.

## Reminders and the message limit
Send at most three messages to the same person about one exception: the first message and two reminders. Space reminders about 30 minutes apart. If three messages have not produced an answer, escalate or hand the exception to the coordinator rather than writing again.

## Holding replies are not approvals
A reply such as "in a meeting", "will look shortly", or "will approve after my 9:30" means the manager has not yet decided. Do not treat it as an approval even if it contains the word approve. Wait, and send a reminder at the follow-up time.

## Recording what the manager said
When a manager clearly approves the time as submitted, record the exception as resolved. When the manager asks for different hours or a different pay code, record a correction request with the requested change so the coordinator can enter it.
